# Overview
The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers’ Rights (PPV&FRA) Act, 2001 is India’s first sui generis legal framework for protecting the rights of farmers and plant breeders, ensuring equitable benefit-sharing and seed sovereignty. It was launched in 2001 under the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare and became operational in 2005.

# Eligibility
Eligibility criteria are not explicitly stated but generally include farmers who have saved, used, sown, resown, exchanged, or shared seeds of registered varieties. Researchers can also benefit from the exemption provided for experimentation and varietal development.

# Benefits
- **Farmers' Rights (Section 39):** Farmers can save, use, sow, resow, exchange, and share seeds of registered varieties; they are also eligible for compensation for non-performance of varieties.
- **Breeders’ Rights:** Exclusive rights to produce, sell, or license protected varieties, ensuring intellectual property protection for innovation.
- **National Gene Fund:** Created to channel benefit-sharing fees and support in-situ conservation and rewarding farmers.
- **Researchers' Exemption:** Allows use of registered varieties for experimentation and varietal development, ensuring open scientific access.
- **Benefit-Sharing & Protection:** Recognition of community knowledge through the National Register of Plant Varieties (NRPV) and legal remedy for biopiracy.

# Funding
The scheme does not specify any direct funding mechanism. However, it mentions the creation of a National Gene Fund to channel benefit-sharing fees and support in-situ conservation and rewarding farmers.
