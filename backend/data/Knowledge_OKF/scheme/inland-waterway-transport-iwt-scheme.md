# Overview

Inland Waterway Transport (IWT) involves the movement of goods and passengers through navigable rivers, canals, backwaters, and creeks using boats, barges, or ferries. It is a fuel-efficient, environment-friendly, and cost-effective mode of transportation.

Key Initiatives to Promote IWT in India:

- **Jal Marg Vikas Project (JMVP):** A World Bank-funded initiative aimed at augmenting the capacity of National Waterway 1 (Ganga). This includes dredging, terminal development, and navigational aids.
  
- **Sagarmala Programme:** Integrated with IWT to boost port-led development and hinterland connectivity.

- **National Waterways Act, 2016:** Declared 111 waterways as National Waterways (NWs) for development.

- **Jal Vikas Marg Society (JVMS):** An institutional mechanism under IWAI for managing World Bank-assisted projects.

- **‘Jalvahak’ Cargo Promotion Scheme (2024):** Provides up to 35% operating cost reimbursement to cargo movers using IWT.

- **Ganga Vilas Cruise and River Tourism Initiatives:** Boosts passenger traffic and tourism potential on National Waterways 1 and 2.

# Benefits

- **Jal Marg Vikas Project:** Enhances navigation capacity through infrastructure development.
- **Sagarmala Programme:** Improves port connectivity and hinterland access.
- **National Waterways Act, 2016:** Provides legal framework for waterway development.
- **Jal Vikas Marg Society:** Facilitates private sector investment in IWT projects.
- **‘Jalvahak’ Cargo Promotion Scheme:** Subsidizes operating costs to encourage cargo movement via IWT.
- **Ganga Vilas Cruise and River Tourism Initiatives:** Promotes tourism and passenger traffic.

# Funding

Funding is provided through various sources, including the World Bank for initiatives like the Jal Marg Vikas Project. The government also supports schemes such as the ‘Jalvahak’ Cargo Promotion Scheme with financial incentives.
